# Theme Development

Not to make this Drupal specific- I have a lot of theme development experience in WordPress and other CMS platforms. I have been working with Drupal for a while now, and I have seen the evolution of theme development in this platform. Between Wordpress and Drupal (other CMSs are pretty different with this approach), I have often been stubborn about using a developer forward or from scratch custom approach. The old argument against using a packaged theme or theme builder; you end up replacing all the code anyway. Now I do have more experience in WP but I have also approved of this approach for Drupal projects and have a few builds under my belt. These builds are *dated*; and even some of my Drupal projects had hard contraints working with other services (such as a book store I worked with in the past where their index of books was maintained with a specific Drupal version and locked down theme provided by a service).

I am now working on something new and 100% my own and when I hit the first dumb roadblock with my dev theme, I scrapped it in favor of a pre-built. It may make sense for me to go back and replace but I am also trying to efficently speed towards a beta and none of the maintainability behind the app requires the level of utility the custom theme provides.

## Problem numero uno

I am hosting a composer integrated version of Drupal and wanted to require the "Gesso" theme. I thought this was right up my alley since I am a painter on the side, I use gesso on my canvases all the time! Like many of the newer dev themes, Gesso comes with a sub-module "Gesso Utility". You can execute the gesso command with composer and it will copy the theme to use your custom namespace. I did this and a warning pops off to remember; if you wish to remove the original gesso theme, uninstall gesso helper, move, rename, and enable again. This worked fine locally but for the life of me I could not get a happy state on the dev server due to it's composer required build process. I spent maybe 4 hours trying to trick dev but then I asked myself what the heck this was for? All to have a blank theme with webpack, sass, and storybook...that's it? 

## Enter Solo

I already decided to move on and upon reviewing other resources, I found the solo theme builder. Immediately, the install page looked obnoxious. A massive theme with tons of regions and infinite options to set colors, fonts, layouts... A bunch of paragraphs for reusable blocks. I am now unblocked to build almost my complete concept without touching code. Is everyone going to know I am using a theme builder? Maybe, but maybe I am also jaded and with enough white space, this looks like any app anayways. My goal is to get to beta. The faster I can get this in the hands of users the better. The belief in this idea is in the content itself and not how it's styled...or built.
